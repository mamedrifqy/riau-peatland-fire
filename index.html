How phases are defined here. Each month is classed by its own ONI value against ±0.5 °C, as coloured in the report. NOAA’s rule needs five consecutive months; this report does not apply it, since August to November 2016 is coloured La Niña after only four. Treat rainfall as a same-month trigger with a short lead

Rainfall’s correlation with hotspots is in the same month, with one month of lead, with two, and close to zero from three months. Rainfall acts fastest in the same month, but a shortfall one or two months earlier still carries signal.

Not tested here: forecast skill, daily rainfall, or a threshold for action. The data are monthly and pooled.

Plan for El Niño years as a higher baseline

El Niño months carry the hotspots of La Niña months. 2015 and 2019 together account for of ten years of hotspots.

Data and method

Where the numbers come from

Every figure on this page comes from one source: the 60-page Laporan Korelasi Iklim, Gambut & Hotspot for 12 kabupaten and kota in Riau (Regional Peatland Report 2026), five pages per regency. The report prints its statistics and draws each monthly series as a chart.

The monthly values here were read from the report’s vector charts, not estimated by eye. Each plotted point was converted to a value using the chart’s axis ticks. Hotspot counts came out as exact whole numbers in all 12 regencies, which is the check that the conversion is right.

The page then recomputed coefficients the report prints (Spearman ρ and p, Kruskal-Wallis H, p and η² for every regency, plus the cross-regency peat correlations). match to the printed precision. The extraction and checks are in build/; run them to reproduce everything.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Kampar and Pekanbaru share one climate series. Their rainfall and temperature are identical in all 120 months, so Pekanbaru is not an independent observation. Their hotspot counts differ.
  • Kuantan Singingi has no mapped peat in the report but 529 hotspots, so hotspot counts are regency-wide rather than peat-only.
  • The SPI proxy is a z-score of monthly rainfall over the 120 months, not a drought index with a fitted distribution and not SPEI. It ranks months exactly as rainfall does.
  • The cross-regency result rests on 12 points, and each point is a regency average. It says nothing about single fires.
  • Monthly resolution. A “zero-lag” result means the same calendar month, which can hide a lead of days or weeks.
  • The report does not name its hotspot, rainfall and temperature products, so this page cannot either. Check the original data pipeline for the product names and versions.
